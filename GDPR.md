# HR & GDPR: Overview

## Relationship

**HR (Human Resources)** manages **employment, payroll, and working conditions**. **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)** governs **how personal employee data is processed legally**. Together, they create a compliance framework for storing, using, and protecting employee information.

---

## HR Core Requirements

| Requirement | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| **Employment Contracts** | Define roles, responsibilities, salary, working hours, start/end dates |
| **Mandatory Disclosures** | Employees must receive written employment terms, benefits, tax information |
| **Working Hours Tracking** | Document hours worked, overtime, leave entitlements |
| **Payroll Records** | Salary, deductions, tax withholding, pension contributions |
| **Health & Safety** | Occupational health records, workplace safety compliance |
| **Termination Documentation** | Severance, exit interviews, final pay, reference policies |

**Retention:** Typically **5–7 years** post-employment (tax/legal requirement).

---

## GDPR Core Requirements

| Requirement | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| **Legal Basis** | Employer must have lawful reason (employment contract, legal obligation, or consent) to process data |
| **Data Minimization** | Collect only data necessary for HR purposes |
| **Transparency** | Inform employees what data is collected, why, and how long it's kept |
| **Special Category Protection** | Medical data, ethnic origin, religious belief—forbidden except for occupational health/safety |
| **Consent** | Marketing, third-party sharing, or non-contractual processing requires explicit employee consent |
| **Right to Access** | Employees can request copies of their personal data |
| **Right to Delete** | Employees can request erasure after employment ends (with exceptions for legal/tax records) |
| **Audit Trail** | Document all data processing activities for regulatory inspection |
| **Data Breach Notification** | Report unauthorized access to employee data within 72 hours |

**Retention:** **7 years** (GDPR default for employment records with tax/legal implications).

---

## Common Violations

| Violation | Why It Breaks Rules |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Storing medical data without occupational health reason** | Special category data without legal derogation (GDPR Art. 9) |
| **Sharing employee data with recruiters without consent** | No legal basis; third-party sharing requires explicit consent (GDPR Art. 6, 7) |
| **Keeping employee records 10+ years** | Exceeds retention period; violates storage limitation (GDPR Art. 5) |
| **No transparency** | Employees unaware why data is collected; violates transparency (GDPR Art. 13) |
| **No audit log** | Cannot prove compliance; violates accountability (GDPR Art. 5, 24) |
| **Processing data without employment contract** | No legal basis; violates lawfulness (GDPR Art. 6) |

---

## Compliance Checklist

- ✅ **Legal basis documented** (employment contract, statutory obligation, or consent)
- ✅ **Data minimization applied** (only necessary fields collected)
- ✅ **Special category data excluded** (unless occupational health/safety reason exists)
- ✅ **Transparency provided** (employees informed via privacy notice at hire)
- ✅ **Retention policy set** (~7 years; delete after employment + grace period)
- ✅ **Audit trail maintained** (timestamped logs of all data access/modifications)
- ✅ **Consent documented** (explicit for marketing, data sharing, non-contractual processing)
- ✅ **Access controls enforced** (only HR staff access employee records)
- ✅ **Breach procedure in place** (72-hour notification protocol)
